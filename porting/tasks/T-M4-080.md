### PR-PROVCLI-158 - Copilot rewind drops the last n turns from the CLI's history and leaves files to the app's checkpoints

Then "session.history.rewind" is requested with {"sessionId":...,"eventId":<id>,"mode":"conversation"} - the literal mode - and the result's "outcome" must be exactly "success", else ProviderError.failed(result.error ?? - **Persistence:** The CLI's own history shrinks.
- **Failure cases:** The two exact messages above.
- **Cancellation:** not cancellable
- **Windows path:** None.
- **Verify by:** interaction test over a successful rewind, too few points and a non-success outcome

### PR-PROVCLI-160 - Copilot builds a session approval per request kind, or refuses to offer one
- **Source:** DroppyCode/Services/Providers/CopilotSession.swift:873-902 - sessionApproval(for:)
- **Preconditions:** A permission request object.
- **Action:** The app decides whether to offer Approve for session.
- **Expected:** The complete table. ### PR-PROVCLI-164 - Copilot stop asks the runtime to shut down and closes the connection anyway after three seconds
- **Source:** DroppyCode/Services/Providers/CopilotSession.swift:312-324 - stop() with its comment: one Task requesting "runtime.shutdown" then closing, and a second Task closing after 3 seconds
- **Preconditions:** Any state.
- **Action:** The thread stops the session.
- **Expected:** isStopping is set first. With no connection, or an already closed one, nothing further happens. Otherwise TWO detached Tasks start: the first requests "runtime.shutdown" with its result discarded and then closes the connection, letting the CLI flush the session's event log; the second sleeps EXACTLY 3 seconds and closes the connection as a backstop. Whichever finishes first closes it; the other close is harmless. Copilot is the only provider in this area with a graceful shutdown request.
- **Side effects:** One RPC round trip; connection close
- **Persistence:** The CLI flushes its own session event log.
- **Failure cases:** A CLI that never answers shutdown is closed by the 3 second backstop.
- **Cancellation:** This IS the stop path.
- **Windows path:** The 3 second backstop is the only thing bounding a hung shutdown, so it must be a real timer and not tied to the closing process (PA-0007).
- **Verify by:** interaction test with a stand-in that never answers runtime.shutdown, asserting the 3 second bound
