### PR-CHAT-053 - A viewport that has ended up past the end of the content snaps back to the end on the next main-queue turn
- **Source:** DroppyCode/App/Views/Chat/ThreadTimeline.swift:521-535 if new.distanceFromBottom < -1, atRest, and the content or container changed shape in this very frame, then DispatchQueue.main.async { withTransaction(unanimated) { position.scrollTo(edge: .bottom) } }
- **Preconditions:** The content shrank under the offset, for instance a finished turn folding its whole transcript into one block while the reader's place was anchored at the top.
- **Action:** The scroll geometry is measured.
- **Expected:** The timeline snaps unanimated to the end. This only fires when the content or the container changed shape in the same frame, so a wheel bounce past the end, which moves only the offset, plays out on its own. The scroll is deferred to the next main-queue turn because a scroll write applied inside the geometry handler re-enters it in the same display pass, an uncapped loop that keeps dirtying the window until the display-cycle watchdog kills the app.
- **Persistence:** None.
- **Cancellation:** not cancellable
- **Windows path:** Every scroll issued from inside a scroll-geometry callback must be deferred one turn. This is a correctness requirement, not a nicety: the source records an app-killing feedback loop without it.
- **Verify by:** interaction test folding a long turn while scrolled to its top

### PR-CHAT-054 - A reader who is pinned to the end but has drifted away from it is snapped back, except during a live resize
- **Source:** DroppyCode/App/Views/Chat/ThreadTimeline.swift:557-572 the last geometry branch - pinned or old.distanceFromBottom < 48, at rest, and abs(new.distanceFromBottom) > 1; guard !liveResize.isActive; sets isPinnedToBottom and anchorsBottomOnGrowth true and scrolls unanimated to the bottom on the next main-queue turn
- **Preconditions:** The content grew or reflowed to a new width, the chat box took height, or the scroll view lost its place.
- **Action:** Geometry is measured while the reader is at rest.
- **Expected:** The timeline snaps back to the end, never slides, so a new row lands in place and fades in on its own. Mid-resize the snap is skipped because the bottom anchor already holds the bottom edge each frame; the settle after the resize does it instead. A reader whose pin flag went stale is caught by the end they held in the previous frame.
- **Persistence:** None.
- **Cancellation:** not cancellable
- **Windows path:** Snap, do not animate. An animated correction here reads as the timeline fighting the reader.
- **Verify by:** interaction test

### PR-CHAT-055 - The reader is counted as scrolling when the offset moves by more than the anchor's own move
- **Source:** DroppyCode/App/Views/Chat/ThreadTimeline.swift:489-494 anchorMove = anchorsBottomOnGrowth ? contentHeight delta : 0; readerMoved = abs((new.offset - old.offset) - anchorMove) > 1; scrolled = isUserScrolling or (not nudging and not arriving and readerMoved and the container height did not change and not coasting)
- **Preconditions:** The timeline is measured on every scroll frame.
- **Action:** The offset changes.
- **Expected:** Content growing under a bottom-held reader moves the offset by exactly the growth and under a top-held one not at all; anything beyond one point is the reader. Wheel ticks, which report no scroll phase at all, are therefore recognised even while a turn streams rows. Movement during the timeline's own nudge, during the arrival hold, while coasting, or in a frame where the container height changed, is not the reader.
- **Persistence:** None.
- **Cancellation:** not cancellable
- **Windows path:** Reproduce the anchor-compensated comparison. The source records that counting wheel ticks only in frames where the content stood still meant a reader's scroll went unrecognised while a turn streamed, the end stayed pinned, and the timeline snapped back under them.
- **Verify by:** interaction test: wheel-scroll up during a streaming turn and assert the view stays put

### PR-CHAT-056 - Accumulated drift past a tolerance tells the lazy list where the viewport is
- **Source:** DroppyCode/App/Views/Chat/ThreadTimeline.swift:496-520 the at-rest branch - shrink = max(0, old.contentHeight - new.contentHeight); drift is the anchor-compensated offset move; noteDrift(shrink + drift, tolerance:) arms a viewport refresh when the running total exceeds the tolerance; a shrink over one point also arms the blank watch; the reader's own scrolling resets the drift | DroppyCode/App/Views/Chat/ThreadTimeline.swift:807-809 jumpTolerance - max(48, viewportHeight / 4) | DroppyCode/App/Views/Chat/ThreadTimeline.swift:1120-1131 noteDrift adds and returns true once past the tolerance, zeroing the total; resetDrift zeroes it
- **Preconditions:** The content changes shape under a resting reader.
- **Action:** Geometry is measured.
- **Expected:** Growth the bottom anchor follows is not counted as drift, since the row that grew is the row under the viewport. Everything else accumulates, because a fold under an animation moves the viewport a little each frame. Once the total passes the greater of 48 points and a quarter of the viewport height, the lazy list is told where the viewport is and the total starts again.
- **Persistence:** None.
- **Cancellation:** not cancellable
- **Windows path:** Only needed where the list virtualizes against a remembered viewport. If the Windows list recomputes visible range from the live scroll offset every frame, this becomes a no-op; say so explicitly rather than porting a dead mechanism.
- **Verify by:** unit over the drift accumulator plus an interaction test folding a turn under an animation

### PR-CHAT-057 - A timeline showing nothing repairs itself in three escalating steps and then stops
- **Source:** DroppyCode/App/Views/Chat/ThreadTimeline.swift:744-777 repairLayout - requires viewportHeight > 0; defers while the reader scrolls, coasts or the rows are frozen, setting repairSkippedWhileScrolling; past the end or pinned-and-away it snaps to the end first; when showing nothing it runs case 0 refreshViewport, case 1 stackGeneration += 1 then arm a refresh, case 2 pin to the bottom, snap and arm a refresh, default return; then blankRepairs += 1 and the blank watch is re-armed | DroppyCode/App/Views/Chat/ThreadTimeline.swift:976-990 setVisible - a row appearing cancels the blank watch and zeroes blankRepairs; the last row leaving arms the watch | DroppyCode/App/Views/Chat/ThreadTimeline.swift:952-955 showsNothing - there are rows to show and none of them is on screen
- **Preconditions:** A layout pass has left the viewport over space the lazy list built nothing for.
- **Action:** The blank watch fires, a stalled scroll phase is cleared, or the reader returns to the front.
- **Expected:** First the list is told where the viewport is. Still blank a beat later, the list is rebuilt from scratch and told again. Still blank, the timeline pins to the end, snaps there and tells the list again. A fourth blank in a row is left alone. Any row coming back on screen ends the sequence and resets the counter.
- **Persistence:** None.
- **Failure cases:** A repair requested while the reader is scrolling is remembered and run when the scroll ends (ThreadTimeline.swift:455-464).
- **Cancellation:** not cancellable
- **Windows path:** Only required where the list virtualizes. The escalation order and the three-attempt cap are part of the contract; an unbounded retry loop is not acceptable.
- **Verify by:** interaction test forcing a blank viewport

### PR-CHAT-058 - The blank watch looks 300 milliseconds later and asks for a check only if still blank
- **Source:** DroppyCode/App/Views/Chat/ThreadTimeline.swift:1099-1106 armBlankWatch - cancels the previous watch, sleeps 300 ms, and bumps checkRequest only when not cancelled and showsNothing
- **Preconditions:** Rows exist and none is on screen, or something changed shape.
- **Action:** The watch is armed.
- **Expected:** Exactly one pending watch at a time; re-arming cancels the previous one. After 300 milliseconds, if rows are still showing nothing, a layout check is requested.
- **Persistence:** None.
- **Cancellation:** Cancelled by a row reporting itself visible, by re-arming, or by the view going away.
- **Windows path:** A single cancellable timer per timeline.
- **Verify by:** unit with a fake clock

### PR-CHAT-059 - A scroll phase whose end is never delivered is cleared by a watchdog with per-phase and absolute ceilings
- **Source:** DroppyCode/App/Views/Chat/ThreadTimeline.swift:1067-1094 notePhase - isUserScrolling for interacting or decelerating, isCoasting for decelerating or animating; an interacting phase resets the drift; the watch polls every 200 ms and ends the phase when the reader has not moved the offset for `still` seconds (2.0 for interacting, 0.4 otherwise) or the phase has run 3.0 seconds; ending the phase withdraws the scroll-activity report and bumps checkRequest
- **Preconditions:** A scroll phase is reported.
- **Action:** The phase's end event is lost, for instance because content grew under an animated scroll or the app went to the back mid-flick.
- **Expected:** The phase is force-ended after the stillness window or the three-second ceiling, whichever comes first, and a layout check is requested. Without it the timeline would count itself as moving for good and never snap or repair again, which the source records as leaving a running turn the reader could not see until the thread was reopened.
- **Side effects:** ScrollActivity.shared is told this timeline has stopped scrolling, which lets shimmers and animations elsewhere resume
- **Persistence:** None.
- **Cancellation:** A new phase report cancels the watch.
- **Windows path:** Windows scroll events differ, but the watchdog is mandatory: any scroll-phase state the port keeps must have an equivalent timeout, with the same 2.0 / 0.4 / 3.0 seconds.
- **Verify by:** unit with a fake clock plus an interaction test that drops the end event

### PR-CHAT-060 - Rows stop answering hover and hit tests while the content moves under the pointer, lifting 150 milliseconds after the last moved frame
- **Source:** DroppyCode/App/Views/Chat/ThreadTimeline.swift:701-719 noteScrollMovement - records the movement time; on the first movement sets isReaderScrolling, reports the activity, cancels the settle watch and starts one that polls every 80 ms and clears the flag once 0.15 seconds have passed with no movement | DroppyCode/App/Views/Chat/ThreadTimeline.swift:721-726 liftScrollFreeze - cancels the watch and clears the flag at once | DroppyCode/App/Views/Chat/ThreadTimeline.swift:2292-2297 ScrollFreeze - content.allowsHitTesting(!tracking.isReaderScrolling) | DroppyCode/App/Views/Chat/ThreadTimeline.swift:442-452 onScrollPhaseChange - .interacting notes movement, every other phase lifts the freeze
- **Preconditions:** The reader drags, flicks or wheels the timeline.
- **Action:** Frames move under a resting pointer.
- **Expected:** The rows stop hit-testing, so SwiftUI does not re-hit-test every row's hover region each frame and flip hover states as rows pass. The freeze lifts 150 milliseconds after the last moved frame, or immediately when a scroll phase other than interacting is reported, so a click that stops a flick always reaches its target. The flag lives on the tracking object and is read only by this leaf modifier, so its flips never re-render the timeline body.
- **Persistence:** None.
- **Cancellation:** The watch is cancelled when the view disappears (endActivity).
- **Windows path:** Windows equivalent is disabling pointer events on the list during scroll. The source measures this as a fifth of the main thread's scroll-time work, so it is a performance requirement, not cosmetic.
- **Verify by:** interaction test: hover a row, wheel-scroll, assert no hover state change until 150 ms after the last tick
