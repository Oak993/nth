### PR-PROVREG-219 - UsageLimitSignal.matches recognises five lowercase substrings in a provider error message
- **Source:** DroppyCode/Services/Providers/UsageLimitSignal.swift:6-10 matches - the text is lowercased once and tested for "usage limit", "hit your limit", "usage_limit", "limit reached" and "out of extra usage"
- **Preconditions:** A provider reported an error.
- **Action:** matches is called.
- **Expected:** True when the lowercased text contains any of those five exact substrings. The test is substring containment, not a word match, so "usage limits" also matches. These five and only these five.
- **Persistence:** None.
- **Failure cases:** None; the function is total.
- **Cancellation:** not cancellable
- **Windows path:** Same five substrings and the same single lowercasing.
- **Verify by:** unit over each substring plus near misses

### PR-PROVREG-220 - UsageLimitSignal.resetTime reads a Unix timestamp after a bar, treating values above 1e11 as milliseconds
- **Source:** DroppyCode/Services/Providers/UsageLimitSignal.swift:12-22 resetTime - the first pattern is a vertical bar, optional whitespace, then nine or more digits; the leading non-digits are dropped and the remainder parsed as a number; a value greater than 1e11 is divided by 1000, with a comment that some providers write milliseconds
- **Preconditions:** An error message such as one ending in a bar and a timestamp.
- **Action:** resetTime is called.
- **Expected:** At least nine digits are required. A value above 100000000000 is treated as milliseconds and divided by 1000; anything at or below is treated as seconds. The timestamp path is tried BEFORE the clock path and wins when both are present.
- **Persistence:** None.
- **Failure cases:** Fewer than nine digits: the pattern does not match and the clock path is tried.
- **Cancellation:** not cancellable
- **Windows path:** Same pattern, the same nine-digit minimum and the same 1e11 threshold.
- **Verify by:** unit over second and millisecond timestamps and short digit runs

### PR-PROVREG-221 - UsageLimitSignal.resetTime also reads a clock time such as resets 3pm, taking the next such time and honouring a parenthesised zone
- **Source:** DroppyCode/Services/Providers/UsageLimitSignal.swift:23-38 resetTime - the case-insensitive pattern is "reset" with an optional trailing s, an optional " at", whitespace, one or two digits, an optional colon and two digits, optional whitespace, then am or pm; the digits are extracted from the match; the minute defaults to zero; pm before 12 adds 12 and am at 12 becomes 0; a parenthesised identifier of the form Area/Location anywhere in the TEXT sets the calendar time zone; the next matching date after now is returned with the nextTime matching policy
- **Preconditions:** An error message containing a clock time.
- **Action:** resetTime is called and the timestamp pattern did not match.
- **Expected:** The returned date is the NEXT occurrence of that hour and minute after now, which may be tomorrow. The am and pm conversion is the standard twelve-hour rule. The time zone is taken from a parenthesised region identifier searched over the whole message, not only the matched clock substring.
- **Persistence:** None.
- **Failure cases:** An hour that does not parse as an integer: nil.; No am or pm marker: no match, so nil.
- **Cancellation:** not cancellable
- **Windows path:** Same patterns. PLATFORM BOUNDARY: the time zone identifier lookup must resolve IANA names, which Windows does not use natively, so a mapping is required.
- **Verify by:** unit with a fixed now over the documented examples

### PR-PROVREG-222 - UsageLimitSignal.format renders today as a short time, tomorrow and within-six-days with a prefix, and anything further out as an abbreviated date
- **Source:** DroppyCode/Services/Providers/UsageLimitSignal.swift:42-52 format - the doc comment gives the three shapes; a date today renders as the shortened time alone; tomorrow renders as "tomorrow at " plus the time; a date earlier than now plus six days renders as the wide weekday name, " at " and the time; otherwise the abbreviated date and shortened time
- **Preconditions:** A reset date.
- **Action:** format is called.
- **Expected:** Exactly four branches in that order. The prefixes are the lowercase literal "tomorrow at " and the literal " at " after a full weekday name. The six-day boundary is measured from now, not from the start of the day.
- **Persistence:** None.
- **Failure cases:** None.
- **Cancellation:** not cancellable
- **Windows path:** Same four branches and the same two literals. Date and time formatting must follow the same styles.
- **Verify by:** unit with a fixed now across the four branches
