## Requirements

### PR-THEME-153 - Streamed text arrives fully laid out and is revealed by a cosmetic veil that changes opacity only
- **Source:** DroppyCode/UI/Markdown/StreamVeil.swift:4-24 the doc: every flush is committed to layout at once and a purely cosmetic veil over the characters that just arrived dissolves over the next beat; nothing moves and nothing resizes; each append registers the new tail as a chunk with its arrival time; a chunk fades exactly once and text already faded never animates again; only opacity changes, and only the paint, so every consumer keeps its layout byte for byte; offsets are in characters of the plain text the consumer hands over
- **Preconditions:** A reply is streaming.
- **Action:** A flush of new text arrives.
- **Expected:** The new text is laid out immediately in its final position and size; nothing reflows as it reveals. A veil over exactly the newly arrived characters dissolves over the following fraction of a second. Text that has already faded never fades again. Offsets are counted in characters (grapheme clusters) of the plain text, so the same veil serves both a styled text object and a native text storage.
- **Persistence:** not persisted
- **Failure cases:** None expressed in source.
- **Cancellation:** not cancellable; see PR-THEME-158 for what happens when a reply stops
- **Windows path:** Grapheme-cluster offsets, never UTF-16 code units: a port indexing UTF-16 misplaces every fade after an astral character (markdown-link-grammar extract, gotcha 4).
- **Verify by:** interaction test: a streaming reply's text does not move as it reveals

### PR-THEME-154 - The veil's fade length follows the stream's cadence: three times an exponential moving average of the gaps, clamped to 120 and 400 milliseconds
- **Source:** DroppyCode/UI/Markdown/StreamVeil.swift:40-44 `static let shortestFade: TimeInterval = 0.12`, `static let longestFade: TimeInterval = 0.40`, `private static let curve = 1.6`, `private static let cadenceSeed: TimeInterval = 0.16`, `private static let gapCeiling: TimeInterval = 1.0` | DroppyCode/UI/Markdown/StreamVeil.swift:73-81 on an append: `let gap = min(now.timeIntervalSince(lastAppend), Self.gapCeiling)`; `cadence = cadence * 0.7 + gap * 0.3`; `let duration = min(max(cadence * 3, Self.shortestFade), Self.longestFade)`; the chunk is recorded with that duration, fixed at arrival | DroppyCode/UI/Markdown/StreamVeil.swift:36-37 the duration is fixed at arrival from the cadence in force then
- **Preconditions:** A veil is attached and text is arriving.
- **Action:** A flush appends text.
- **Expected:** The gap since the previous append is capped at one second. The cadence is updated as 0.7 times its previous value plus 0.3 times that gap, starting from a seed of 0.16 seconds. The new chunk's fade duration is three times the cadence, clamped between 0.12 and 0.40 seconds, and is fixed for the life of that chunk; later cadence changes do not alter it. The first append uses the seed, giving a duration of 0.40 seconds (0.48 clamped down).
- **Persistence:** not persisted
- **Failure cases:** A pause longer than a second is treated as a one-second gap, so one long pause does not slow every later fade.
- **Cancellation:** not cancellable
- **Windows path:** All five constants are exact. The duration is per chunk and immutable; only the boost (PR-THEME-155) changes a chunk's effective speed after arrival.
- **Verify by:** unit: the cadence recurrence and the clamp, including the first-append case

### PR-THEME-155 - Three or more chunks fading at once speed every chunk up by 30 percent each, retroactively
- **Source:** DroppyCode/UI/Markdown/StreamVeil.swift:129-132 `private static func boost(_ active: Int) -> Double { 1 + 0.3 * Double(max(0, active - 2)) }` with the doc "A backed-up stream (three or more chunks fading at once) dissolves faster." | DroppyCode/UI/Markdown/StreamVeil.swift:88-95 spans(): the boost is computed from the current chunk count and multiplies elapsed time for every chunk | DroppyCode/UI/Markdown/StreamVeil.swift:124-127 prune(): a chunk is dropped when its boosted elapsed time reaches its duration | DroppyCode/UI/Markdown/StreamVeil.swift:109-113 settlesAt: each chunk's settle time is its start plus its duration divided by the current boost
- **Preconditions:** Several chunks are fading at once.
- **Action:** A frame is painted, or the backlog drains.
- **Expected:** With n chunks in flight, every chunk's elapsed time is multiplied by 1 plus 0.3 times (n minus 2), floored at 1, so two or fewer chunks run at normal speed and each extra chunk speeds all of them up by 30 percent. The multiplier applies to elapsed time already accumulated, not only to time from now on. When the backlog drains the multiplier drops, so a chunk's computed progress can go backwards and its text can visibly get fainter again. This is the shipped behaviour.
- **Persistence:** not persisted
- **Failure cases:** The backwards ramp described above; no guard exists against it.
- **Cancellation:** not cancellable
- **Windows path:** Reproduce the retroactive form exactly: applying the boost only to newly elapsed time would remove the backwards ramp and change how a fast stream looks.
- **Verify by:** unit: progress for a fixed chunk decreases when the chunk count falls from 4 to 2

### PR-THEME-156 - A chunk's opacity follows one minus the remaining progress raised to 1.6
- **Source:** DroppyCode/UI/Markdown/StreamVeil.swift:88-95 spans(): `let progress = min(1, max(0, now.timeIntervalSince(chunk.started) * boost / chunk.duration))` then `Span(range: chunk.range, alpha: 1 - pow(1 - progress, Self.curve))` | DroppyCode/UI/Markdown/StreamVeil.swift:42 `private static let curve = 1.6` | DroppyCode/UI/Markdown/StreamVeil.swift:14-16 doc: "The dissolve eases as (1 − p)^1.6, so the text shows through at 1 − veil."
- **Preconditions:** A chunk is dissolving.
- **Action:** A frame is painted at a given time.
- **Expected:** Progress is the boosted elapsed time over the chunk's duration, clamped to 0...1. The text's opacity over that chunk is one minus (one minus progress) raised to the power 1.6, so the reveal starts fast and eases into the final value. A chunk at progress 0 is fully transparent and at progress 1 fully opaque.
- **Persistence:** not persisted
- **Failure cases:** None expressed in source.
- **Cancellation:** not cancellable
- **Windows path:** Exact exponent 1.6; a linear fade reads visibly differently.
- **Verify by:** unit: alpha at progress 0, 0.5 and 1

### PR-THEME-157 - A veil attaching to text already on screen adopts it without fading, and a rewrite re-veils only the changed tail
- **Source:** DroppyCode/UI/Markdown/StreamVeil.swift:52-85 advance(): the first call stores the text and returns without registering a chunk; a changed text has its common prefix with the previous text computed; every chunk is clipped to that prefix and dropped when clipping would leave it empty; when the new text is longer than the prefix a chunk is registered for the range from the prefix to the end | DroppyCode/UI/Markdown/StreamVeil.swift:63-72 the comment: a rewrite shorter than a chunk's start drops that chunk whole, because a range with its upper bound below its lower one traps | DroppyCode/UI/Markdown/StreamVeil.swift:18-21 doc: what is already on screen when a veil attaches is adopted as the baseline and never fades; a rewrite that is not an append keeps the common prefix's fades and re-veils only the changed tail | DroppyCode/UI/Markdown/StreamVeil.swift:52-55 the method is idempotent for unchanged text, so a body may call it on every evaluation
- **Preconditions:** A row scrolls back into view mid-reply, or the parser rewrites a block.
- **Action:** The veil is fed the current text.
- **Expected:** The first text a veil ever sees is adopted silently: nothing fades. Afterwards, a change is diffed against the previous text by common character prefix. Chunks are clipped to that prefix and any that would become empty are dropped, so a rewrite that shortens the text cannot produce an invalid range. Everything after the prefix is registered as one new chunk. Feeding the same text again does nothing at all.
- **Persistence:** not persisted
- **Failure cases:** A rewrite shorter than a chunk's start drops that chunk; without the guard the range arithmetic would trap.
- **Cancellation:** not cancellable
- **Windows path:** The adoption rule is what stops a whole reply flashing in when the reader scrolls back to it. The clipping guard is a real crash fix.
- **Verify by:** unit: a first advance registers no chunk; a truncating rewrite drops the affected chunks without error

### PR-THEME-158 - A veil keeps its baseline while the reply streams and forgets everything once it has finished
- **Source:** DroppyCode/UI/Markdown/StreamVeil.swift:102-107 step(): feeds the text only while active, and reports whether anything is still dissolving | DroppyCode/UI/Markdown/StreamVeil.swift:115-122 reset(): clears the text, the chunks and the last-append time, and returns the cadence to its seed | DroppyCode/UI/Markdown/StreamVeil.swift:291-307 settledText(): `if !veiled { veil.reset() }`; with segments the text is rebuilt as `Text("\(out)\(t)")` for a fixed run and `Text("\(out)\(Text(a))")` for a prose run, else `source.text`; the comment: once the reply has finished the veil is emptied too so a settled paragraph keeps no copy of itself, while it still streams the baseline stays or a quiet beat between two flushes would make the next one arrive unveiled | DroppyCode/UI/Markdown/StreamVeil.swift:273-279 the body chooses the fading text while anything is dissolving and the settled text otherwise
- **Preconditions:** A paragraph has been streaming.
- **Action:** The chunks all settle, with the reply either still streaming or finished.
- **Expected:** While the reply is still streaming, a paragraph whose chunks have all settled renders as plain text but keeps its baseline, so the next flush fades in rather than snapping. Once the reply is no longer marked as streaming, the veil is reset: it drops the text copy, the chunks, the last-append time, and returns the cadence to 0.16. A settled paragraph therefore holds no second copy of its own text.
- **Side effects:** Releases the veil's copy of the paragraph text
- **Persistence:** not persisted
- **Failure cases:** None expressed in source.
- **Cancellation:** not cancellable
- **Windows path:** Resetting only when the message is no longer veiled, rather than when the chunks empty, is load-bearing: resetting on empty would make every flush after a pause arrive unveiled.
- **Verify by:** unit: a pause between flushes keeps the baseline; the end of a reply clears it

### PR-THEME-159 - One shared display clock drives every veiled text, runs at a preferred 30 frames a second, holds through a scroll, and tears itself down
- **Source:** DroppyCode/UI/Markdown/StreamVeil.swift:156-160 doc: one display-rate clock for every veil on screen; a veiled text reads the clock only while it dissolves, so each tick re-renders exactly the paragraphs still fading; the link is torn down the moment the last of them has settled; the text stays a bare text in its stack so a list marker keeps its first-baseline alignment mid-stream | DroppyCode/UI/Markdown/StreamVeil.swift:171-181 run(until:): the deadline is extended to the later of the current one and the new one; a display link is created from the main screen when none is running; `link.preferredFrameRateRange = CAFrameRateRange(minimum: 24, maximum: 60, preferred: 30)` with the comment that a veil is a short opacity ramp, not motion, so half the display's rate halves the per-frame rebuilds; the link is added in the common run-loop mode | DroppyCode/UI/Markdown/StreamVeil.swift:183-193 tick(): returns without updating while the timeline is scrolling, so veiled paragraphs are not rebuilt for frames the reader never sees; otherwise the clock advances and, once past the deadline, the link is invalidated and cleared | DroppyCode/UI/Markdown/StreamVeil.swift:281-289 the fading text keeps the clock running until the last chunk's settle time and paints at the real time, so the first frame after a flush is never stale
- **Preconditions:** At least one paragraph is dissolving.
- **Action:** Frames pass, the reader scrolls, and the last chunk settles.
- **Expected:** One clock serves every dissolving paragraph. It runs with a preferred rate of 30 frames a second, a minimum of 24 and a maximum of 60. While the timeline is scrolling the clock holds its last value, so nothing repaints for frames the reader will not see, and the ramp resumes when the scroll ends. Each paragraph extends the clock's deadline to its own last settle time; when the deadline passes the clock is torn down. A dissolving paragraph paints at the real current time, not at the clock's value, so the frame right after a flush is never stale.
- **Side effects:** Holds a display-synchronised callback while anything is dissolving
- **Persistence:** not persisted
- **Failure cases:** No main screen available: no clock is started, and the dissolving paragraphs will not repaint until something else re-renders them.
- **Cancellation:** The clock stops itself when its deadline passes.
- **Windows path:** Platform boundary: a composition-synchronised callback at the same preferred rate. The scroll gate needs whatever the port uses to know the timeline is scrolling. Do not wrap the text in a per-frame timeline view: the source notes that would break list-marker baseline alignment mid-stream.
- **Verify by:** interaction test: scrolling during a stream does not repaint fading paragraphs; the clock stops when the reply settles

- **Cancellation:** not cancellable

### PR-THEME-161 - The native paragraph's veil paints temporary colours on the layout manager, keeping links in the accent, and is torn down when the view leaves
- **Source:** DroppyCode/UI/Markdown/StreamVeil.swift:341-343 doc: the same chunks painted as temporary foreground colours on the layout manager, which change no glyph and no line; a display link repaints while anything is dissolving and is torn down the moment nothing is | DroppyCode/UI/Markdown/StreamVeil.swift:353-367 update(): feeds the view's string while active; while anything fades it paints and starts a display link on the view in the common run-loop mode; otherwise it settles, keeping the text only while still active | DroppyCode/UI/Markdown/StreamVeil.swift:376-392 tick(): holds while scrolling, tears down when the target view is gone, repaints while fading and settles otherwise | DroppyCode/UI/Markdown/StreamVeil.swift:394-407 settle() invalidates the link, clears the painted attributes and resets the veil when the text is not being kept; clear() removes the temporary foreground attribute over the intersection of the painted range and the current storage length
- **Preconditions:** A link paragraph is streaming.
- **Action:** Frames pass and eventually the view is removed.
- **Expected:** The veil is applied as temporary colours on top of the laid-out text, so no glyph, line or layout changes. Each run keeps its own colour, only thinner: a link run is drawn in the theme accent at the span's alpha, a prose run in its own colour or the label colour. The previously painted range is cleared before each repaint and once the veil settles, intersected with the storage's current length so a shortened text cannot be over-cleared. A display link attached to the view drives the repaints, holds through a scroll, and is invalidated as soon as nothing is fading or the view has gone. Removing the view detaches the veil, which stops the clock and forgets the text. Unlike the shared text clock, this one is not pinned to a preferred frame rate.
- **Side effects:** Holds a display-synchronised callback per streaming link paragraph
- **Persistence:** not persisted
- **Failure cases:** Missing layout objects: painting and clearing are skipped.
- **Cancellation:** Detaching the view stops the clock and clears the paint.
- **Windows path:** Platform boundary: a temporary per-range foreground colour that does not invalidate layout. The difference in frame-rate pinning between this clock and the shared one is upstream behaviour (markdown-link-grammar extract, gotcha 11).
- **Verify by:** interaction test: a streaming link paragraph's links stay accent-coloured as they fade in; the clock stops when the row scrolls away
