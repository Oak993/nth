Glass UI

The nth main window as it looks today, and rebuilt in iOS 27 Liquid Glass. Every glass surface is a live WebGL2 lens over what is really behind it: the wallpaper, the thread and the panels under it. Only the containers are glass; the controls on them are glyphs and labels.

1. Glass is what it does to the backdrop

The body is a clear frost with a 6% white lift. Only bright content is pulled down; mid-tones keep at least 85% of their brightness, and the tint takes out colour, not light. Over orange it stays orange, over near-black it is a lifted grey sheet.

2. One line of light, one dark edge

A hairline one device pixel wide catches the light on the side facing it, with a small glint where the light is square on and a faint bounce opposite. Inside the outline sits the iOS 27 darkened band. No border, bevel or gloss.

3. A lens you can see

The outer 4.5 px stay sharp (blur 1.2 px), so content just outside the rim visibly squeezes and curves into it, and the bezel magnifies by about 1.1× before it flattens out. Full frost is reached 10 px in. Try the lens test grid.

4. No glass on glass

Both versions carry the app's real controls: a bare paperclip, the voice orb, a text model chip, the effort ring and send. Today wraps send in a glass circle; Liquid glass makes it the one solid object, a flat silver disc. The composer light runs round both.

5. Readable on anything

A soft dimming appears under text only when the frost behind it gets bright, and it grows when you turn the frost down. Sidebar labels get a soft local shadow, so they hold contrast over the colour blobs without darkening the whole surface.

6. Calm motion

Pressed glass swells a little and brightens on a spring with about 0.7 damping. Toggle knobs turn into a clear lens while they move. Reduced motion drops the bulge and stretch. Uniform scroll-edge bands sit under the title bar and under the composer.

Default glass parameters per look

Values at the composer, in logical px. Frost is the blur radius (Gaussian sigma is about 0.48 of it). Refraction is the bend at the very edge as a multiple of the bezel. Other surfaces scale from these (table below). The Transparency slider blends between the three looks.
